Real-Time Speaker Verification on a Cortex-M4: Bit-Exact 8-Bit Deployment, Measured End to End

Kranthikumar Putta. Preprint, 2026.

PDFCode (TinyVoiceprint)

Speaker verification on microcontrollers has, to our knowledge, so far lacked a system that enrols new voices on the device, runs in real time, and is competitive on a standard benchmark. We present TinyVoiceprint, a real-time, text-independent speaker verifier on an STM32F407 (Cortex-M4, 168 MHz) that enrols a new voice on the device and decides every 0.4 s from 1.5 s of audio from the board's own microphone. A 139k-parameter time-channel-separable network runs in 8-bit integer arithmetic, with its convolutions bit-exact against a reference, and takes 293 ms per decision, with about 30 KB of network RAM, 151 KB of weights and 17.5 mJ above a 122 mW listening floor. On VoxCeleb1-O it reaches 7.98% EER over whole clips (17.7% from a single 1.5 s window), against 28.50% for the closest earlier microcontroller system on the same trials. Replaying the test set through the board shows that the remaining gap lies in the small model's robustness to the acoustic channel, not in the microphone: a large teacher loses under one point, our network 4.6, and training with real noise and room echo cuts this to 3.3 at no extra cost. These results show that on-device enrolment and verification fit within a microcontroller's budget, and identify channel robustness as the main target for closing the gap to larger models.
