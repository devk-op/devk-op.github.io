TinyVoiceprint: Real-Time Text-Independent Speaker Verification with On-Device Enrollment on a Cortex-M4

Kranthikumar Putta. Preprint, 2026 (version 5, November 22).

PDFCode (TinyVoiceprint)

We present TinyVoiceprint, a text-independent speaker verifier that enrolls new voices and runs in real time on an STM32F407 microcontroller (Cortex-M4, 168 MHz), evaluated on VoxCeleb1-O and through the board's own microphone. Every 0.4 s it averages the voiceprints of up to three consecutive 1.5 s speech windows and compares them with the enrolled voice. A 139k-parameter time-channel-separable network runs its convolutions in 8-bit integer arithmetic, bit-exact with a reference implementation and checked at boot; pooling and the output layer use float32. It takes 293 ms per window under the live audio load, about 30 KB of network RAM and 151 KB of weights, and adds about 17.5 mJ per decision to a 122 mW listening floor. On VoxCeleb1-O the integer network reaches 8.02% equal error rate (EER) over whole clips and 11.5% with 2.3 s test segments against whole-clip enrollment; on the same trials, the published models of the closest earlier microcontroller system score 28.50%, a gap that our design steps attribute largely to training data and recipe. On 400 test clips replayed through the board, a 20.8M-parameter teacher loses about one EER point while our network, before real-noise training, loses 4.4, consistent with the remaining gap lying in the small model's robustness to the acoustic path; training with recorded noise and room echo reduces the loss to 3.5 points at no inference cost. In simulated on-device decisions on the replayed clips, the deployed threshold accepts 22.9% of impostor decisions and rejects 8.0% of target decisions, so the system suits personalization rather than authentication.
